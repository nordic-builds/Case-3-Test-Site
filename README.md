# Interviewer Guide

This page is for the interviewers only. It holds the script, the consent form, the questionnaires and the interview questions for each session.

## Session at a glance

The session takes about 33 minutes on Microsoft Teams. One of us is the moderator and speaks to the participant, and one is the observer and takes notes without intervening.

| # | Step | Where | Min |
|---|------|-------|-----|
| 1 | Welcome | Spoken | 2 |
| 2 | GDPR check and recording | Spoken | 2 |
| 3 | Think-aloud explanation | Spoken | 2 |
| 4 | Scenario and the three tasks | Spoken + prototype | 11 |
| 5 | UES-SF questionnaire (link 1) | Google Form | 2 |
| 6 | Interview | Spoken | 4 |
| 7 | Observer questions | Spoken | 2 |
| 8 | EMCB questionnaire (link 2) | Google Form | 2 |
| 9 | Debrief and renewed consent | Spoken | 4 |

## Before the session

The participant receives an email in advance. It must contain:

- The date, time and Teams meeting link, and the guide for sharing the screen in Teams.
- Their participant ID.
- The link to the [GDPR form](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSeok2nbN-E6R2zKpRNv0_kGEClY4ZbMz7Vtx2fFvwpjUFEtdQ/viewform?usp=dialog) (information, age group, gender, children and written consent), to be completed before the session.
- Link 1 ([UES-SF](https://forms.gle/wfFPgBoJKJLGPKWj8)) and link 2 ([EMCB](https://forms.gle/AoGbdH4jq9maggZE7)), with the line: "Please do not open them yet, only when we ask you to during the meeting."
- A note to join from their own computer with a microphone, from a quiet place.

Moderator: have the prototype link of the participant's condition (A to D) ready to send in the Teams chat.

## Script

### 1. Welcome (2 min)

> "Hi, and thank you for your time. I'm \[name\], and this is \[name\], who will be taking notes. We're Information Science students at Aalborg University Copenhagen, and this is part of our student project.
>
> Today we're testing the search function of an online shop prototype that we built ourselves. It isn't connected to any real shop, and no real purchases are made. The session takes about 30 to 35 minutes. You'll do three short shopping tasks, fill in two short questionnaires and answer a few questions about your experience. There are no right or wrong answers. We're testing the shop, not you. Taking part is voluntary, and you can stop at any time without giving a reason.
>
> Before we start, I'll explain one thing about how we'd like you to work."

### 2. GDPR check and recording (2 min)

**Check the information sent beforehand.** Ask:

> "Did you fill in the GDPR form with the information and the consent, and did you have time to read the information? Do you have any questions?"

If not, share the [GDPR form link](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSeok2nbN-E6R2zKpRNv0_kGEClY4ZbMz7Vtx2fFvwpjUFEtdQ/viewform?usp=dialog) in the chat and give the participant a moment to read and fill it in now. No written consent, no test.

**Ask before recording:**

> "We'd like to record your screen and your voice for the whole session, not your face. Are you okay with that?"

**Start the recording.** Only after a yes: *"I'm starting the recording now. Only your screen and your voice are recorded, not your face."* The interviewer starts the recording. If the participant says no or is unsure, there is no recording and no test.

### 3. Think-aloud explanation (2 min)

> "While you do the tasks, we'd like you to think out loud. That means saying whatever goes through your mind as you go: what you're looking for, what you notice, why you click something, and what you find confusing or easy. Just talk as if you were alone, and don't worry about explaining yourself or sounding smart. If you go quiet, I may remind you to keep talking. I won't answer questions or help during the tasks, but that's only so everyone gets the same experience."


### 4. Scenario and the three tasks (about 11 min)

Read in a natural, relaxed tone. **Don't mention sustainability.**

> "Imagine you've decided to get a bit more organised with your everyday routine, and you're taking lunch and drinks with you during the week. You open the online shop to pick up a few things you'd really use. You'll look for three things, one at a time, and I'll tell you what. Choose whatever you'd really pick if you were buying it for yourself."

Send the prototype link of the participant's condition in the Teams chat. Read the tasks aloud one at a time, always in this order:

1. Buy a water bottle.
2. Buy a lunch box to take to work, school or out.
3. Buy a food container to store leftover food in the fridge or freezer.

The interviewer stays silent, observes and takes notes: what is typed, which suggestion is clicked and its position, which product is bought. If the participant is quiet for a while: *"Please keep talking."* Do not help and do not answer questions about the tasks. The observer notes anything to ask about in step 7.

After the last task, say: *"Thank you, that was the shopping part. Now we have a short questionnaire."*

### 5. UES-SF questionnaire (2 min)

> "Please go back to the email and open link 1. The first question asks for your participant ID. Fill it in now, and tell me when you're done."

If the participant can't find the email, share [link 1](https://forms.gle/wfFPgBoJKJLGPKWj8) in the chat. The interviewer stays quiet. The items are in the UES-SF section below.

### 6. Interview (4 min)

The audio is already being recorded. Ask the three open questions, then let the participant talk. Use silence.

1. "How did you find searching in this shop?"
2. "How did you decide what to pick?"
3. "Was there anything that stood out to you?"

Probing short or vague answers ("it was fine", "easy"). Use one of these:

- "Can you say more about that?"
- "What made it feel \[easy / fine / confusing\]?"
- "Can you give me an example from one of the tasks?"
- "What were you thinking at that moment?"

Don't suggest answers and don't mention sustainability yet.

### 7. Observer questions (2 min)

> "\[Name\], is there anything you'd like to ask?"

The observer asks extra questions based on what they noticed during the tasks (a hesitation, a repeated search, an unexpected choice). Keep them neutral and open, and don't reveal the purpose of the study.

### 8. EMCB questionnaire (2 min)

> "Thank you. One more short questionnaire. Please go back to the email and open link 2. Again, the first question asks for your participant ID. Tell me when you're done."

The items are in the EMCB section below. If the participant can't find the email, share [link 2](https://forms.gle/AoGbdH4jq9maggZE7) in the chat.

### 9. Debrief and renewed consent (4 min)

Read the debriefing:

> "Thank you very much. We would now like to tell you more about the study. We did not tell you everything at the start, because it could have influenced how you shopped. The study is about sustainable shopping. Some participants saw search suggestions for sustainable products, such as stainless steel or plastic-free products, mixed into the list, placed at the top, or highlighted in green. Others saw the normal suggestions. We want to find out whether the way suggestions are shown influences what people click on and choose. Your choices were not right or wrong, and they will only be analysed together with those of other participants."

Then ask:

1. Did you notice anything about the search suggestions while you were shopping?
2. How do you feel about search suggestions being used to steer your choices in this way? Do you find it helpful, neutral or disturbing?
3. Would you accept this kind of suggestion in a real online shop? Why or why not?
4. Do you have any questions about the study?

**Renewed consent**, a spoken yes on the recording:

> "Now that you know the purpose, do you still agree that your data, including this recording, may be used as described in the information we sent you? Please say yes or no out loud."

If the participant withdraws, delete the recording and all their other data the same day. Otherwise: *"That's the end of the session. I'm stopping the recording now. Thank you very much."*

### Information text

> **What is the study about?** We are a group of students in Information Science at Aalborg University Copenhagen. As part of our student project, we are testing the search function of an online shop prototype. You will search for and buy three products in the prototype. The prototype is modelled on the Føtex online shop, but we built it ourselves and it is not operated by or affiliated with Føtex or Salling Group. No real purchases are made. Some details of the study will only be explained at the end of the session, because knowing them in advance could influence how you shop.
>
> **Your data.** We record your clicks and choices, your screen (not your face), and your voice while you think aloud and during the interview. You also fill in two short questionnaires. We do not collect your name together with this data: you receive a participant ID. The recording of the session is made in Microsoft Teams \[check where it is stored and for how long\], and we move it to our own secure storage and delete it from Teams. Your clicks and mouse movements on the prototype are also recorded by a log in the prototype and by the analytics tool Microsoft Clarity \[check where Clarity stores the data and for how long\]. Your data are used only for our student project and are only accessible to the four group members. Quotes in our report are anonymised. All data that could identify you are deleted on \[date\] at the latest.
>
> **Your rights.** Taking part is voluntary. You can stop at any time without giving a reason. Until \[date\], you can ask to see, correct or delete your data, or withdraw your consent, by contacting \[name, email address\]. The four group members (Rosanna Graumann, Francesca Cristiani, Inês Galiza da Silva Pinto and Alexis Walhovd) are responsible for your data as private individuals; Aalborg University is not. You can complain to the Danish Data Protection Agency (Datatilsynet).

### Questions in the form

1. Participant ID (given by us).
2. Age group: 18–29 / 30–49 / 50+
3. Gender: Female / Male / Non-binary / Prefer not to say
4. Do you have children? Yes / No
5. Please tick everything you agree to:

- [ ] I have read and understood the information above, and I have had the opportunity to ask questions.
- [ ] I am 18 years or older.
- [ ] I agree to take part in the study and know that I can stop at any time.
- [ ] I agree that the screen is recorded during the shopping tasks.
- [ ] I agree that my voice is recorded while I think aloud during the shopping tasks.
- [ ] I agree that the interview is audio-recorded. **(optional)**
- [ ] I agree that anonymised quotes from the interview may be used in the report. **(optional)**

Without the first five boxes, the test does not start.

## UES-SF questionnaire

Form: [UES-SF Google Form](https://forms.gle/wfFPgBoJKJLGPKWj8)

Google Form, opened after the tasks. The participant types the participant ID and rates 12 statements from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). Say: *"Please think about the search you just used, the search bar and its suggestions."* Participants open it only when asked.

1. I lost myself in this experience.
2. This search was attractive.
3. I was absorbed in this experience.
4. I felt frustrated while using this search.
5. My experience was rewarding.
6. I found this search confusing to use.
7. This search was aesthetically appealing.
8. I felt interested in this experience.
9. Using this search was worthwhile.
10. This search appealed to my senses.
11. Using this search was taxing.
12. The time I spent using this search just slipped away.

The items are in a fixed, shuffled order. Statements 4, 6 and 11 are scored in reverse (6 minus the answer). The scale is the User Engagement Scale Short Form (O'Brien, Cairns, & Hall, 2018), adapted to "this search".

## EMCB questionnaire

Form: [EMCB Google Form](https://forms.gle/AoGbdH4jq9maggZE7)

Google Form, opened after the interview. The participant types the participant ID and answers 10 statements about the products they buy in everyday life. Say: *"The next statements are about the products you buy in everyday life. Choose the answer that describes you best."*

Scale: 1 never true, 2 rarely true, 3 sometimes true, 4 mostly true, 5 always true.

1. When there is a choice, I always choose the product that contributes to the least amount of environmental damage.
2. I have switched products for environmental reasons.
3. If I understand the potential damage to the environment that some products can cause, I do not purchase those products.
4. I do not buy household products that harm the environment.
5. Whenever possible, I buy products packaged in reusable or recyclable containers.
6. I make every effort to buy paper products (toilet paper, tissues, etc.) made from recycled paper.
7. I will not buy a product if I know that the company that sells it is socially irresponsible.
8. I do not buy products from companies that I know use sweatshop labor, child labor, or other poor working conditions.
9. I have paid more for environmentally friendly products when there is a cheaper alternative.
10. I have paid more for socially responsible products when there is a cheaper alternative.

The score is the sum of the 10 answers (10 to 50), with no reverse-coded items. The scale is the Ethically Minded Consumer Behavior scale (Sudbury-Riley & Kohlbacher, 2016).
